Pune picnic and hike spots: how to choose a day out near the city

Hills, lakes and forts surround Pune. Here is how to pick a picnic or short hike, check conditions and stay safe, without relying on one name.

ZikPe editorial 3 min read

Why Pune is easy to escape

Pune sits near the Sahyadri hills, with lakes, forts and green valleys within a couple of hours. Many residents head out for a day to cool off, walk and eat. For a visitor, it is a gentle way to see the countryside.

Pick one place and take your time.

Picnic or hike

A picnic needs a flat, shaded spot, water and a place to sit. A hike needs a trail, sturdy shoes and more planning. Decide which you want, since the two need different places and different preparation.

If you have children or older relatives, a simple picnic is easier.

Check the season

Winter is pleasant and dry. Summer is hot, and midday walking can be hard. The monsoon turns the hills green, but it also brings slippery trails, swollen streams and sudden fog. Local authorities sometimes restrict access to some forts, waterfalls and dams in the rains.

Check current local advisories before you go.

Check the access

Ask a local, your hotel or a reliable local source about the road, the parking and any entry fees. Some places can be reached only on rough roads. Weekends can be crowded and traffic can be slow on the way back.

Leave early to beat the traffic.

What to carry

Carry water, snacks, a hat, sunscreen, a rain jacket, a small first-aid kit, a power bank and a bag for your litter. Wear shoes with grip. Download an offline map, since mobile signal can be weak.

Tell someone where you are going and when you expect to return.

Walking safely

Keep to marked trails, avoid cliff edges and slippery rock and do not swim in unknown water. Turn back if the weather changes. Avoid walking after dark, and go with a companion.

If you are unsure, hire a local guide.

Respect the place

Take your litter home, avoid loud music and do not light fires. Many places are used by villagers, farmers and herders. Ask before taking photographs of people and homes.

Leave the place as you found it.

Eating along the way

Small roadside stalls often serve simple snacks and tea. Choose busy places, and drink sealed or filtered water. If you have a picnic, keep food covered and in a cool bag.

Carry back what you carry out.

Plan the day

Start early, walk or picnic, eat a long lunch and be back on the road before dusk. Leave time for rest and for delays. A slower day is usually a better one.

Keep your plan flexible, since weather and traffic change.

A sample day

Leave Pune by eight, reach a lakeside or hill spot by ten, walk for an hour and settle in for a picnic in the shade. Rest through the early afternoon, take a short second walk, and begin the drive back by four to beat the traffic.

The best days are the ones with a little slack in them.

Questions to ask before you go

Is the road open and in good condition? Are there any entry rules or fees? Is there drinking water, a toilet and a place to buy food? What is the weather forecast? Is the place safe for the group you are travelling with?

Write the answers in a note and share them with someone at home.
