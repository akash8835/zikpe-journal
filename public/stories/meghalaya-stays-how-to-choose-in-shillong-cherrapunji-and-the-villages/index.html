Meghalaya stays: how to choose in Shillong, Cherrapunji and the villages

Meghalaya offers hotels, homestays and village stays. Here is how to think about location, season, access and what to check before you book.

ZikPe editorial 3 min read

A wet, green state

Meghalaya is known for its hills, waterfalls, living root bridges and heavy rainfall. That shapes where you stay, how you travel and what you pack. Rain and mist can be beautiful, but they also affect roads and views.

Check the season and a current forecast before you book.

Shillong as a base

Shillong is the state capital and has the widest choice of hotels and guesthouses. It is a practical base for day trips, with shops, restaurants and transport. Traffic can be busy at peak times.

If you like city comforts, start here.

Cherrapunji and Sohra

The Cherrapunji area is known for its rain, waterfalls and cave and root-bridge walks. Stays range from simple guesthouses to small resorts. Clouds can hide views, so allow a flexible schedule.

Ask your host about walking routes, the weather and safe times for treks.

Village homestays

Homestays in villages such as those near living root bridges can offer a quieter experience. Facilities are often simple. Check whether there is hot water, a power backup and a mobile signal.

Ask what is included, such as meals, and how to get there.

Getting around

Roads in the hills can be slow and sometimes affected by landslides in the rains. Many visitors hire a local driver for a day or longer. Agree the price and the plan in advance.

Allow extra time between places.

Check before you book

Look at recent reviews, photos and the exact location. Ask about the road up to the property, since some stays need a walk. Confirm check-in times, meals and cancellation terms in writing.

A clear answer before booking avoids surprises.

Respect local culture

Many communities in Meghalaya have their own customs. Ask before photographing people and homes, follow guidance at sacred sites and keep to marked paths. Carry out your litter.

Visitors who behave with care are welcomed more warmly.

Cash and connectivity

Phone signals can be weak in villages, and card machines are not everywhere. Carry some cash for small purchases, and download maps in advance.

A little planning keeps the trip smooth.

Permits and rules

Rules for visitors can change, and some areas may have their own requirements for access or fees. Check current guidance with your host or the state tourism department before you go.

Keep a copy of your ID and booking details with you.

Packing for the hills

Pack a rain jacket, quick-dry clothes, sturdy shoes with good grip, a torch, insect repellent and a small first-aid kit. Keep electronics in waterproof bags.

Layers help, since evenings can be cool.

Seasons in brief

The monsoon brings heavy rain and lush scenery but can disrupt roads. Cooler months can have clearer views. Each season has its charm, and your choice depends on what you value.

Ask your host what to expect during your dates.

Food and meals

Local meals often feature rice, dal, vegetables and, in many places, meat and fish dishes. Ask your host about what is served and whether they can adjust for dietary needs. In villages, meals may be fixed and need notice.

Tell your host about allergies and preferences when you book.
