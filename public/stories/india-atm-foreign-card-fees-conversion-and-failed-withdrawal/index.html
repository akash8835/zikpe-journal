UPI for visitors

Using an ATM in India with a foreign card: fees, conversion and a failed withdrawal

Separate the issuer’s charges, the machine’s fee and currency conversion, then keep evidence if cash does not arrive.

ZikPe editorial 4 min read

An ATM logo is not the complete answer

Before travelling, ask your card issuer whether international cash withdrawals are enabled and what limits apply. A card that works for purchases may have different conditions for cash. Check the actual card and account, not a general statement about the network.

At the machine, read the displayed terms and make sure the location and surroundings feel suitable. If something looks altered or suspicious, do not use it. Keep your PIN private and do not accept assistance that requires another person to handle the card or enter it for you.

Separate the costs before comparing

An international withdrawal can involve a fee from the machine operator, an issuer cash-withdrawal charge and a foreign-currency conversion cost. Those are different lines, even when only one appears prominently on screen.

Do not apply an Indian savings-account free-transaction allowance or domestic fee cap to every foreign-issued card. Ask your issuer for the terms that cover your account. Credit-card cash withdrawals can also carry cash-advance charges and interest under the card’s conditions.

Home-currency conversion is a separate offer

A machine may offer to bill the withdrawal in your home currency. That is dynamic currency conversion, not simply a language option. Read the local-currency amount, conversion rate and markup before choosing.

Declining the machine’s conversion lets the transaction proceed in rupees when supported, with conversion handled under your card’s terms. It does not promise a fee-free withdrawal or a universal cheapest rate. Compare the disclosed offer with the terms of your own card rather than choosing because a familiar currency feels easier.

If cash does not arrive, stop and record

A debit notification without dispensed cash is a problem to report, not a reason to keep pressing the same request. Keep the receipt or error message, date, time, amount and ATM identifier. Check whether the app shows a pending entry or a completed debit.

Contact your card issuer promptly through its official channel and use the machine operator’s legitimate support details where needed. Never send a PIN, password or one-time code to someone claiming to speed up the refund.

Let the issuer explain the resolution

Ask how a missing-cash or partial-cash dispute is handled for your card, what evidence is needed and how to follow its progress. Domestic Indian reversal rules are not a safe promise about every overseas issuer’s investigation.

Reconcile the outcome before making a second withdrawal to replace missing cash. A later reversal and a new withdrawal are separate transactions. Keep the records until the account is correct, and plan the day without depending on one machine working exactly as expected.
