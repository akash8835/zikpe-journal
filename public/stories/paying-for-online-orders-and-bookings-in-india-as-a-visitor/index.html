Paying for online orders and bookings in India as a visitor

Booking trains, buying online or ordering delivery in India can ask for details visitors do not have. Here is how to plan for it.

ZikPe editorial 3 min read

What can be tricky

Some Indian websites and apps expect an Indian mobile number, an Indian bank account or a local payment method. Others take international cards but may ask for extra verification. The experience varies by site.

Check payment options early, before you rely on a booking to be easy.

Train and bus bookings

Indian railways and many bus operators sell tickets online. Foreign visitors can face account and payment limits, which differ by service. Check the official site for the current rules for foreign tourists.

If online booking is hard, a travel agent, your hotel or a station counter may help. Ask about any service fee first.

Shopping and delivery

Many shopping and delivery apps accept cards, cash on delivery or Indian payment methods. A foreign card may or may not work, and delivery to a hotel may need your room number and phone.

Read the payment screen carefully, and check the total, delivery fee and any taxes before you confirm.

Verification codes

Some payments ask for a one-time code sent to your phone. If you are roaming or using an eSIM, check you can receive texts before you try. Do not share codes with anyone.

If a code does not arrive, wait and try again rather than repeating the payment many times.

Hotels and tours

Hotels and tour operators may ask for a deposit. Use official websites or well-known platforms, and read the cancellation terms. Keep the confirmation email and any receipts.

Be careful about paying in advance to individuals through personal accounts.

Spotting scams

Be cautious of unexpected links, requests for urgent payment and prices that seem too good. Check the website address and read reviews. A reputable business will have clear contact details.

If something feels wrong, stop and check with your hotel or bank.

Keep records

Take screenshots of confirmations and receipts. Save them in an offline folder. If there is a problem later, you will have what you need.

A little tidiness now saves a lot of time later.

A backup plan for bookings

If an online booking does not work for you, do not spend hours trying. Ask your hotel to help, use a reputable travel agent or book in person at a station or office. A small service fee can save a lot of stress.

Always ask about the fee and the refund terms before you agree.

Before you press pay

Check the total, the date, the names and the cancellation rules. Make sure the website address is correct and the connection is secure. If you are on public Wi-Fi, consider waiting for a safer connection for any payment.

A short pause before you press pay is the cheapest protection you have.

Time zones and deadlines

Booking windows, cut-off times and refund deadlines are often in Indian time. Check the time zone when you book, and set a reminder on your phone for any deadline that matters. A missed cut-off can cost a booking or a refund.

Keep the confirmation email handy, along with the booking reference.
