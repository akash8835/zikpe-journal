Issues withdrawing cash from ATMs in India, and how to handle them

Cards declined, machines out of cash, money debited with nothing dispensed: calm steps to take when an ATM does not go as planned.

ZikPe editorial 3 min read

Why it can go wrong

ATMs can be empty, offline or set up differently from machines at home. Your card may also be limited by your bank for security or by the machine's own rules. Most problems are small and fixable if you stay calm.

This guide gives general steps. Your bank's rules and the ATM owner's process decide the final outcome.

Keep both banks' phone numbers saved before you travel.

Card declined

A decline can mean the machine does not accept your card type, the amount is above a limit, your bank blocked the card or the network is down. Try a smaller amount, try another machine and check your banking app for alerts.

If your bank blocked the card, contact its international number. Do not share your PIN with anyone who calls you.

A second card in a separate place saves the day.

Machine out of cash or out of order

Machines in smaller towns and on festival weekends can run empty. If a machine shows an error, do not keep trying. Walk to a bank branch or a busy area and try there.

If you must withdraw before a long trip, do it a day early.

Carry enough small notes for a day so one empty machine does not stop you.

Debited but no cash

Sometimes the account is charged but no notes come out. Keep the receipt if one prints, and note the date, time and machine location. Contact the bank that owns the ATM and your own bank soon after.

Refund timelines depend on the banks involved. A written, specific report moves faster.

Check your account again after a few days to see whether the amount was reversed.

Card kept by the machine

If the machine swallows your card, do not leave without telling someone. Contact the bank that owns the machine, usually shown on the front, and your own bank to block the card.

Ask the staff at a nearby branch for help. Do not accept help from strangers.

Keep a backup payment method so you are not stuck.

Protect yourself

Cover the keypad when you enter your PIN, check that nothing is attached to the card slot and avoid machines in dark spots. Ignore anyone who offers to help.

Review your statement after the trip for any strange charges.

Small habits prevent most of the bigger problems.

What to note down

When a withdrawal fails, note the date and time, the amount, the machine's location and the bank name on it. Take a photo of the screen and the receipt if there is one. These details make a report much easier to follow.

Send your report in writing where you can, and ask for a reference number.

Keeping a calm backup

A second card, kept in another bag, and a little emergency cash in the hotel safe cover most failures. Save both banks' phone numbers offline on paper.

With a backup in place, a failed ATM is a small delay, not a crisis.

When to contact whom

If the machine belongs to a bank, its name and a help number are usually on the front. Call that bank about the machine and your own bank about your card. If your card is lost or kept, block it at once.

Keeping the right numbers in your notes saves time when you are tired or stressed.
